My subject is the sensory routine at Yun Gui Xiang Rice Noodles. It is a daily shelter where heat, sourness and steam hold my restless life in place. But this refuge is quietly slipping away, it's not just through city redevelopment or changing menus, but through sensory memory itself. The exact sting of the chili oil and the taste of the noodles, both fade the moment I step back onto the street. Across the twenty records, a repeating ritual of emotional recalibration shows up. Each visit logs the interplay between the soup itself: its colour, its numbing intensity and my inner mood. Whether I was seeking comfort after a draining project or quietly marking a small personal win, ordering this familiar bowl became a grounding anchor. Repetition, then, is not monotony but a survival mechanism, where small shifts in spice mirror small shifts in how I cope. In the end, this archive can keep visual snapshots, recorded shop noise, and written notes on spice levels, but it cannot hold the fleeting warmth that leaves with the steam. The exact weight of tiredness left at the table, the lingering taste of comfort, and the silent, invisible boundary the shop briefly builds between me and the outside world, none of it can be captured.
